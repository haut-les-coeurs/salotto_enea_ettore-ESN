%% 5–6 · ACEY DEUCEY
\startpage\label{acey}
\gameheader{1}{ACEY DEUCEY}{In or out? Three cards and a cool head}{3–8 players}{10–20 minutes}{1}{enea}
\textbf{The idea.} The dealer turns over two cards. You bet that the third will have a value \textbf{between the two}. With a 4 and a J, for example, you win with a 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 or 10. Suits don’t matter; \textbf{the Ace is always high}.
\secz{Set-up}
Everyone receives \textbf{10 chips} and puts \textbf{1 in the centre}: these chips make up the \textbf{shared pot}. The dealer shuffles the 52 cards, without jokers. The player to their left starts.
\secz{Your turn, step by step}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The dealer turns over \textbf{two cards in front of you}. If they have the same value or are consecutive, such as 6 and 7, the dealer discards them and turns over two new ones.
 \item You can say \textbf{‘Pass’}: you pay nothing and it’s the next player’s turn. Or you say how much you are betting, for example \textbf{‘I bet 2’}. For now, keep those chips in front of you.
 \item The dealer turns over the \textbf{third card}. Compare it with the first two and pay or collect as in the examples below. Then discard the cards and pass on to the next player.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{center}
\examplecell{36mm}{\handm{4/C/n,J/D/n,9/S/u}}{\textbf{In between: you win.}\\Take 2 from the pot.}\hfill
\examplecell{36mm}{\handm{4/C/n,J/D/n,2/H/u}}{\textbf{Outside: you lose.}\\Put 2 in the pot.}\hfill
\examplecell{36mm}{\handm{4/C/n,J/D/n,J/S/u}}{\textbf{Same value: ‘post’.}\\Put 4 in the pot.}
\par\vspace{1mm}{\footnotesize In the examples you bet 2. If you win, you keep your 2 chips too.}
\end{center}
\begin{callout}
\textbf{Don’t add up the cards}: only the order of the values counts. Before playing, read the betting limits on the next page.
\end{callout}
\startpage
\bigtitle{Acey Deucey in practice}{how much to bet and how to carry on}
\secz{How much can you bet?}
The minimum bet is \textbf{1 chip}. You must observe two limits:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{The pot must be able to pay you.} If it holds 5 chips, you can’t bet more than 5.
 \item \textbf{You must also be able to cover the post.} To bet 2 you need at least 4 chips: 2 for the bet and another 2 if the third card matches one of the first two.
\end{itemize}
So, if you have 7 chips, you can bet at most \textbf{3}: you would need 8 to bet 4. If you have fewer than 2 chips, you can only pass.
\secz{A turn with a bet of 2}
You have \textbf{8 chips} and there are \textbf{5} in the pot. A 4 and a J come up. You say ‘I bet 2’ and keep your chips in front of you. The dealer turns over the third card:
\begin{itemize}
 \item A \textbf{9} comes up: it lies between 4 and J. You take 2 chips from the pot and keep yours: you now have \textbf{10}.
 \item A \textbf{2} comes up: it lies outside the range. You put 2 of your chips in the pot: you are left with \textbf{6}.
 \item A \textbf{J} comes up: it’s a post. You put 4 chips in the pot in all: you are left with \textbf{4}.
\end{itemize}
\secz{When it’s the others’ turn}
After your turn, the used cards go on the discards. The dealer turns over two new ones for the next player, clockwise. After one turn each, \textbf{dealer included}, the round is over and the role of dealer passes to the left.
\secz{End of the game}
After \textbf{three rounds}, divide the pot among those still in the game. Any remainders go, one each, starting from the dealer’s left. The player with the most chips wins; if the totals are equal, the win is shared. If only one person is left, the game ends at once and they take the pot.
\begin{callout}
\textbf{Empty pot:} everyone adds 1 chip; anyone who can’t drops out.\\
\textbf{Pack nearly finished:} if fewer than three cards remain, reshuffle the discards before the turn.\\
\textbf{Ace and 2:} any card from 3 to King wins; another Ace or another 2 is a post.
\end{callout}

%% 7–8 · VENTUNO
\startpage\label{ventuno}
\gameheader{2}{TWENTY-ONE}{Blackjack: beating the banker without going bust}{2–8 players}{20–40 minutes}{1}{ettore}
\textbf{The idea.} Get as close as possible to \textbf{21} without going over it, beating the \emph{banker}, who is one of the players. You play against the banker, not against each other. The role of banker passes to the left every hand, so everyone has the same advantage.
\secz{What the cards are worth}
\begin{center}
\examplecell{34mm}{\hand{7/S,4/D}}{\textbf{2–10}\\Their number}\hfill
\examplecell{38mm}{\hand{K/H,Q/C}}{\textbf{Jack, Queen, King}\\10 points}\hfill
\examplecell{38mm}{\hand{A/D}}{\textbf{Ace}\\1 or 11}
\end{center}
Suit doesn’t matter. An Ace counts as 11 if that doesn’t make you go bust, otherwise 1. 
\secz{Set-up}
Everyone starts with \textbf{20 chips}. In every hand the stake is fixed: \textbf{2 chips} each. One player is the banker; choose them by lot. The player to the banker’s right cuts the pack.
\begin{callout}
\textbf{Blackjack} = an Ace plus a 10, J, Q or K in the first two cards. It beats a 21 made with more cards and pays \textbf{3 to 2}: staking 2, you take back your 2 and win 3.
\end{callout}
\secz{Dealing and checking}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The players put 2 chips in front of them. The banker deals \textbf{two face-up cards} to each player and two to themselves: one face up and one face down.
 \item With an Ace or a ten-value card showing, the banker checks the face-down card. If it makes Blackjack, the banker shows it and collects the stakes. A player who also has Blackjack takes back their own 2 chips: it’s a draw.
 \item If the banker doesn’t have Blackjack, they pay any players’ Blackjacks: a player who has already been paid \textbf{asks for no more cards and isn’t paid again}.
\end{enumerate}

\startpage
\secz{Twist or stick?}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
 \item Starting from the banker’s left, each player chooses \textbf{‘Twist!’}: the banker adds a face-up card. They may ask for more or say \textbf{‘Stick!’}, stopping there. Anyone who goes over 21 loses their stake immediately, even if the banker then goes bust.
 \item The banker reveals the face-down card: \textbf{draws below 17 and sticks on 17 or more}, even with an Ace and a 6.
 \item Each player still in compares their own total with the banker’s. The banker collects or pays out of their own chips, following the table.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2mm}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{@{}m{50mm}m{62mm}@{}}
\rowcolor{tint}\textbf{If…} & \textbf{then…}\\
You go bust & you lose the 2 chips staked\\
The banker goes bust, you don’t & you take back 2 and win another 2\\
Your total is higher & you take back 2 and win another 2\\
The totals are equal & you take back your 2: a draw\\
Your total is lower & you lose the 2 chips staked\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{center}
\examplecell{53mm}{\hand{A/S,K/D}}{\textbf{Blackjack: +3} net.}\hfill
\examplecell{53mm}{\hand{10/H,6/C,8/D}}{\textbf{24: bust, you lose your stake (−2).}}
\end{center}
\secz{Doubling and handling the pack}
After your first two cards you can \textbf{double to 4 chips}: you receive just one card, then you stop. You win or lose 4; a 21 with three cards is not a Blackjack. \textbf{Reshuffle all the cards between hands}, never during a hand.
\begin{callout}
	\textbf{Why ‘Black Jack’?} Twenty-One began as a parlour game in eighteenth-century Europe. According to legend, the American name comes from a special prize (10 to 1!) paid to anyone who held the Ace of Spades with a black Jack; historians are divided, but the name stuck and the prize didn’t.
\end{callout}
\vfill
\enea{From \textbf{17 up}, it is usually best to stick. With \textbf{11 or less}, twist without fear: you can’t go bust. Between 12 and 16, look at the banker’s face-up card: if it is low (\textbf{2 to 6}), it often pays to stick and let the banker take the risk.}

%% 9–11 · POKER
\startpage\label{poker}
\gameheader{3}{POKER}{Five-card draw: the king of the speakeasies}{2–6 players}{30–60 minutes}{2}{enea}

\textbf{The idea.} Make the best hand of \textbf{five cards}, with a single draw. You use the \textbf{full 52-card pack}, without jokers. To win you can hold the best cards… or convince everyone else to fold.

\vfill
\secz{Set-up}
\textbf{20 chips each}; the dealer role passes to the left every hand. The first bet and every raise are \textbf{1 chip}, with a maximum of \textbf{three raises in total per betting round}.
\secz{Poker terms}
\begingroup\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{17mm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}
 Check & you stay in without betting (if nobody has bet).\\
 Bet & you’re the first to bet in the round: put 1 chip in the pot.\\
 Call & ‘I’ll see you’: you match the highest bet of the round.\\
 Raise & ‘I raise’: you match the highest bet, then add 1 more chip.\\
 Fold & you drop out and leave in the pot whatever you have already put in.\\
\end{tabularx}\endgroup
\secz{A hand, step by step}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Ante.} Before receiving their cards, everyone puts 1 chip in the centre of the table. Then the dealer deals \textbf{5 face-down cards} to each player: look only at your own.
 \item \textbf{First betting round:} the player to the dealer’s left starts. Play continues clockwise until everyone has acted and matched the last bet, or has folded. If everyone checks, you move on anyway.
 \item \textbf{Draw:} starting from the dealer’s left, each player still in the game chooses \textbf{0 to 3 cards} to hand back face down to the dealer and receives the same number from the pack.
 \item \textbf{Second round:} same rules; the first player still in the game to the dealer’s left opens.
 \item \textbf{Showdown:} those who remain show their cards; the best hand wins (ranking on the next page). If only one player is left, they collect at once without showing anything.
\end{enumerate}

%% 11 · SPAREGGI E CAMBIO
\startpage
\secz{Who wins with the same combination?}
Compare the \textbf{values}, starting from the most important card of the combination:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Pair, three of a kind, four of a kind, full house:} the highest value in the combination wins (three 9s beat three 7s; in a full house the three of a kind counts).
	\item \textbf{Two pair:} first the higher pair, then the second, then the remaining card.
	\item \textbf{Straight, flush, high card:} the highest card wins. The straight A-2-3-4-5 is the lowest, 10-J-Q-K-A the highest.
	\item \textbf{Identical hands:} the pot is split. Suits are never used to break a tie.
\end{itemize}
\secz{An example of a draw}
You are dealt these cards. You have two Aces: \textbf{keep the pair}, discard the other three cards and draw.
\begin{center}
\examplecell{55mm}{\handm{A/S/u,A/D/u,7/C/d,4/H/d,2/S/d}}{\itshape Before: keep the two Aces.}\hfill
\examplecell{55mm}{\handm{A/S/n,A/D/n,A/H/u,9/C/u,K/D/u}}{\itshape After: three Aces!}
\end{center}
\secz{When does the game end?}
A dealer rotation is complete when everyone has dealt once. You play \textbf{three rotations}; players who have dropped out are skipped. To start a hand you need \textbf{at least 9 chips}: they cover the ante and up to 4 chips in each of the two betting rounds. Anyone with fewer drops out, keeping their balance. The game ends early if fewer than two players remain. The player with the most chips wins; if tied, the win is shared.
\secz{How much to call?}
\begin{callout}
\textbf{Example of a call:} you’ve already put 1 chip in during the round and the total to call has risen to 3? You add only \textbf{2}. The ante doesn’t count as a bet in the round.
\end{callout}
\vfill
\ettore{Bluffing has a price: if someone calls, the cards decide. You don’t need to raise every turn; choose when to take a risk and keep a poker face.}

%% 12–14 · GIN RUMMY
\startpage\label{gin}
\gameheader{4}{GIN RUMMY}{The elegant duel: draw, discard, meld}{2 players}{30–45 minutes}{3}{ettore}
\textbf{The idea.} With \textbf{10 cards}, form sets and runs and reduce the points of your \textbf{deadwood}, the cards left outside the melds. When there are only a few points left, you can ‘knock’ and end the hand. It’s played \textbf{by two}, up to 100 points.

\secz{Set-up}
A \textbf{52-card pack without jokers}. It’s a game for two. Choose the first dealer by lot, then take turns. \textbf{The dealer plays too}: there is no banker.
\secz{Card values}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Ace = 1}, always low.
 \item \textbf{2 to 10}: their number.
 \item \textbf{Jack, Queen and King = 10}.
\end{itemize}
\secz{The melds}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Set:} 3 or 4 cards of the same value (such as \cardref{7}{\sS}, \cardref{7}{\sH}, \cardref{7}{\sD}).
 \item \textbf{Run:} at least 3 cards of the same suit in sequence (such as \cardref{9}{\sC}, \cardref{10}{\sC}, \cardref{J}{\sC}, \cardref{Q}{\sC}). The Ace goes only \textbf{at the start}: A-2-3 is valid, Q-K-A is not.
\end{itemize}
A card can belong to \textbf{only one meld}. The total of your deadwood tells you when you can knock: \textbf{it is not added to your game score}. The points you earn are worked out on page \pageref{gin-esempio}.
\secz{Dealing and first move}
The dealer deals \textbf{10 cards each}, turns one over to start the \textbf{discards} and leaves the rest face down as the \textbf{stock}.

The player who did not deal may take the face-up card or refuse it. If they refuse, the dealer may take it. Whoever takes it \textbf{discards and ends their turn}. If both refuse, the non-dealer draws from the stock and discards: then normal turns alternate.

\startpage
\secz{A normal turn}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Draw}: add a card to your hand, choosing between the top card of the face-down stock and the one on top of the discards.
 \item \textbf{Discard}: choose one of your cards and place it face up on the discards. If you took from the discards, you can’t put the same card straight back.
\end{enumerate}
After drawing you have 11 cards; after discarding you’re back to \textbf{10}. Don’t lay your melds on the table until someone knocks.
\label{gin-esempio}
\secz{Knocking and Gin}
If, after discarding, your deadwood is worth \textbf{10 points or less}, you can declare ‘Knock!’ and show your hand. With \textbf{zero deadwood points} you make \textbf{Gin}. You aren’t obliged to knock as soon as you can.
\begin{center}
\examplecell{32mm}{\hand{7/S,7/H,7/D}}{\textbf{Set}}\hfill
\examplecell{40mm}{\hand{9/C,10/C,J/C,Q/C}}{\textbf{Run}}\hfill
\examplecell{40mm}{\hand{A/D,3/H,5/S}}{\textbf{Deadwood: 1\,+\,3\,+\,5\,=\,9}}
\end{center}
With this hand you can knock: the three deadwood cards are worth 9.
\secz{Laying off after the knock}
After you knock, your opponent shows their hand and forms their melds. They can then \textbf{add their deadwood to your melds}: for example a K\sC{} to the run above, or a 7\sC{} to the set. This is called ‘laying off’ and reduces their deadwood. You \textbf{cannot add cards to their melds}. With Gin, however, \textbf{nobody can lay off}.
\secz{How points are scored}
In this version, Gin and undercut both carry a \textbf{25-point bonus}. Only the winner of the hand scores:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Winning knock:} if, after the lay-offs, you have fewer deadwood points than the other player, you score the \textbf{difference}.
 \item \textbf{Gin:} you score your opponent’s deadwood \textbf{plus 25}; you win even if the other player has zero deadwood.
 \item \textbf{Undercut:} without Gin, if the other player is left with deadwood \textbf{equal to or lower than} yours, they score the difference \textbf{plus 25}.
\end{itemize}

\startpage
\secz{End of the hand and of the game}
If the player who draws leaves \textbf{two cards in the stock} and then discards without knocking, the hand is void: no points, reshuffle and the same dealer deals again. Otherwise, the dealer role changes every hand. The first player to reach or pass \textbf{100 points} wins the game.
\secz{Scoring examples}
\begin{callout}
You knock with \textbf{9}, the other player is left on \textbf{14}: you score 14\,−\,9\,=\,5. If instead they go down to \textbf{6}, they score 9\,−\,6\,+\,25\,=\,28. At \textbf{9 against 9}, the other player scores 25.
\end{callout}
\secz{A lay-off that changes the result}
In the example on page \pageref{gin-esempio} you knock with 9. After forming their melds, the other player is left with \cardref{7}{\sC}, \cardref{K}{\sC}, \cardref{2}{\sH} and \cardref{2}{\sC}: 21 points. They lay off the 7 on your set and the King on your run: they are left with only \textbf{4 points}. They undercut you and score 9\,−\,4\,+\,25\,=\,30. That’s why knocking carries a risk!

\secz{A little strategy}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Discard isolated court cards}: they are worth 10 points each.
 \item Keep pairs and neighbouring cards of the same suit: they can become melds.
 \item Watch what the other player picks up from the discards: a neighbouring card could help them.
 \item Don’t knock just because you can: if the other player has little deadwood, you risk an undercut.
\end{itemize}
\secz{Quick recap}
\begin{callout}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Ten cards each. Draw, discard and keep your melds in your hand.
 \item With at most 10 points of deadwood you can knock.
 \item The other player can lay off: compare deadwood \textbf{after} the lay-offs.
 \item Gin blocks lay-offs and always wins; the undercut rewards the player who didn’t knock. Both give a 25-point bonus.
 \item Add up the hands’ scores until someone reaches 100 points.
\end{enumerate}
\end{callout}
\vfill
\ettore{Knocking is a matter of timing: you close before the other player improves, but you grant them a final lay-off. Gin takes more patience and denies your opponent that chance.}

%% 15–17 · CUORI
\startpage\label{cuori}
\gameheader{5}{HEARTS}{The art of not taking points}{4 players (or 3)}{60–90 minutes}{3}{enea}
\textbf{The idea.} Here you win by scoring the \textbf{fewest points}. Every \textbf{Heart} you take is worth \textbf{1 point}, the \textbf{Queen of Spades} is worth \textbf{13}: the other suits are worth nothing. It’s a game of ‘tricks’: the aim is to dodge them with style. Each hand is worth 26 points in all, to be handed out (with a heavy heart) among the players.

\begin{callout}
\textbf{What is a trick?} Everyone plays one card each. The winner collects all the cards played: any with Hearts or the Queen of Spades give them penalties. ‘Leading’ means playing the first card of the trick.
\end{callout}
\secz{Set-up}
With \textbf{four} players, each for themselves, using 52 cards without jokers. The dealer deals \textbf{13 cards each} and the role passes to the left every hand. The 2 is low, the Ace high. \textbf{There is no trump suit}: the highest card of the suit led wins the trick.
\secz{Passing cards}
Everyone chooses \textbf{3 cards from their own hand} and gives them face down to the player indicated below. They receive three other cards: they may look at them only after handing over their own. The direction changes every hand:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item hand 1: pass to the \textbf{left};
	\item hand 2: to the \textbf{right};
	\item hand 3: \textbf{across the table};
	\item hand 4: \textbf{no passing}, then start over.
\end{enumerate}

\secz{The first trick}
The holder of the \textbf{2 of Clubs} must lead with that card. The others must play a Club, if they have one: this is called \textbf{‘following suit’}. A player with no Clubs plays a different card, but \textbf{not a Heart or the Queen of Spades}, unless they have only cards of those two kinds left.

\startpage
\secz{Subsequent tricks}
\begin{itemize}
 \item The winner of the previous trick \textbf{leads} the new one.
 \item You must \textbf{follow suit}, if you can. Only if you have no card of that suit may you play a different one, even a penalty card: it cannot win that trick.
 \item The highest card of the \textbf{suit led} wins: whoever plays it collects all the cards in the trick, with their points.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\examplecell{30mm}{\handm{8/C/n}}{\itshape Leads}\hfill
\examplecell{30mm}{\handm{3/C/n}}{\itshape Follows}\hfill
\examplecell{30mm}{\handm{K/C/u}}{\itshape Wins}\hfill
\examplecell{30mm}{\handm{5/H/n}}{\itshape No Clubs}
\end{center}

\textbf{Example after the first trick.} The K\sC{} is the highest Club \textbf{among the cards played}: it also takes the 5\sH{} and therefore 1 point. An Ace of Clubs would have beaten the King.
\secz{‘Broken’ Hearts and the Queen of Spades}
\begin{itemize}
 \item You cannot \textbf{lead with a Heart} until someone has played a Heart in an earlier trick, unless you have only Hearts. From then on Hearts are ‘broken’.
 \item In this version, the \textbf{Queen of Spades alone does not break Hearts}.
 \item After the first trick, you may lead the Queen of Spades; to discard it on another suit, however, you must be \textbf{unable to follow} that suit.
\end{itemize}
\secz{End of the hand}
After the 13 tricks, everyone counts the points collected: \textbf{1 per Heart}, \textbf{13 for the Queen of Spades}. Check: everyone’s points must add up to \textbf{26} in total.

\secz{Shooting the Moon}
If one player takes \textbf{all the Hearts and the Queen of Spades}, they sweep the hand: they score \textbf{0 points} and each of the other three scores \textbf{26 points}.

\startpage
\secz{End of the game}
At the end of each hand, add the penalties to the previous totals. If anyone reaches or passes \textbf{100 points}, the game ends: the player with the \textbf{fewest points} wins. If there is a tie for the lowest score, the win is shared.
\secz{Scoring example}
\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2.5mm}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.45}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\rowcolor{tint}& \textbf{Anna} & \textbf{Bruno} & \textbf{Carla} & \textbf{Dario}\\
Hand 1 & 4 & 13 & 8 & 1\\
Hand 2 & 0 & 7 & 6 & 13\\
Hand 3 & 12 & 0 & 14 & 0\\\hline
\textbf{Total} & \textbf{16} & \textbf{20} & \textbf{28} & \textbf{14}\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
No one shoots the Moon here: every hand adds up to 26. Dario leads with 14.
\secz{With three players?}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Remove the \textbf{2 of Diamonds}: 51 cards remain. Deal \textbf{17 each} and play \textbf{17 tricks}.
 \item Pass three cards to the \textbf{left}, then to the \textbf{right}, then \textbf{none}; repeat this cycle of three hands.
 \item The \textbf{2 of Clubs} always leads. The other rules don’t change.
 \item When someone shoots the Moon, they score 0 and the other \textbf{two} score 26 each: a total of 52. Without the Moon the total stays at 26.
\end{itemize}
\secz{Tips for getting started}
\begin{itemize}
 \item When passing, try to \textbf{void a suit}: you will be able to shed penalty cards when that suit is led, subject to the restrictions of the first trick.
 \item Keep a few low cards to avoid dangerous tricks. Not all tricks hurt: those without penalties are worth zero.
 \item Keep track of the Queen of Spades and watch whether anyone is collecting all the Hearts: they might be going for the Moon.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\ettore{It isn’t enough to lose a trick: you have to lose the right one. Before playing a high card, check how many Hearts are already on the table.}

%% 18–19 · BONUS RAMINO
\startpage\label{ramino}
\gameheader{BONUS}{RUMMY}{A simple variant, with two packs and four jokers}{2–5 players}{30–45 minutes}{2}{ettore}
\begin{callout}
{\suitfont\color{golddk}★}\quad\textbf{Bonus game: you need two packs!} Ask another table to join forces: shuffle the two packs together, \emph{including the 4 jokers}. The more packs, the more fun.
\end{callout}
\textbf{The idea.} Rummy is the forefather of the ‘melding’ games: already widespread in the early twentieth century, it gave rise to the Gin Rummy you have just learned. The game is simple: form sets and runs and be the first to get rid of all your cards. This is our \textbf{simplified variant}.
\secz{Set-up}
\textbf{108 cards}: two 52-card packs plus four jokers. From 2 to 5 players, \textbf{13 cards each}, to be kept hidden from the others. The dealer is chosen by lot and changes every hand, passing to the left. The player to their left starts; play proceeds clockwise.

Turn over one card to start the \textbf{discards}. If it’s a joker, find a different card and then shuffle the jokers back into the remaining face-down pack: this will be the \textbf{stock}.
\secz{The melds}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Set:} 3 or 4 cards of the same value, of \textbf{different suits}. Two 9s of Spades cannot be in the same group.
 \item \textbf{Run:} at least 3 cards of the same suit in sequence. Ace at the start (A-2-3) or at the end (Q-K-A); K-A-2 is not valid.
 \item \textbf{Joker:} stands in for a card; at most \textbf{one per meld}. Declare which card it represents.
\end{itemize}
\begin{center}
\examplecell{52mm}{\hand{9/S,9/H,9/D}}{\textbf{Set of 9s}, different suits}\hfill
\examplecell{58mm}{\hand{4/C,5/C,JK/Z,7/C}}{\textbf{Run}: the joker is the 6\sC{}}
\end{center}

\startpage
\secz{A turn}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Draw}: add a card to your hand, choosing between the top card of the face-down stock and the one on top of the discards.
 \item \textbf{Lay down}, if you wish: place a set or a run in front of you so that everyone can see it. You can lay down more than one. \textbf{No minimum number of points is needed}.
 \item If you have already laid down, even in this turn, you can \textbf{add} cards to your own or others’ melds, keeping them valid: a 6\sC{} to the run 7-8-9\sC{}, or the fourth suit to a set. Groups cannot exceed four cards.
 \item \textbf{Discard}: put one of your cards on top of the discards, \textbf{if you haven’t already gone out}; not the one you have just taken from the discards. Then it’s the turn of the player on your left.
\end{enumerate}
\secz{Going out and counting}
A player who is left with no cards, by laying them down, adding them to melds or discarding the last one, \textbf{goes out and scores 0}. In this variant \textbf{you can go out without discarding}. The others count only the cards left in their hand:
\begin{center}\small\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3mm}\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}m{45mm}C{25mm}@{}}
\rowcolor{tint}\textbf{Card} & \textbf{Points}\\
2 to 10 & their number\\
Jack, Queen, King & 10\\
Ace & 11\\
Joker & 20\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Play \textbf{three hands} and add up the penalties: the \textbf{lowest} total wins. In the event of a tie, the win is shared.
\secz{Rules of our variant}
\begin{itemize}
 \item Jokers already laid down \textbf{cannot be retrieved}; melds on the table are neither dismantled nor rearranged.
 \item If the stock runs out, reshuffle the discards except the last one. If there are no cards to reshuffle, the player whose turn it is may take the last discard; if they don’t want it, the hand ends and \textbf{everyone counts the cards they have left}, with no winner of the hand.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\enea{If you’re playing with four or five, try to \textbf{lay down early}: it gives you the chance to add cards to the melds on the table and unload your hand. If you wait too long, you risk being stuck with lots of points in your hand.}
